Introduction

The nightstand is a small piece that does a lot of work. It holds the lamp you reach for last thing at night, a glass of water, a book, a phone, and it is at eye level every time you lie down. Get it right and the bedside feels calm and ordered; get it wrong and the clutter has nowhere to go.

In a Japandi bedroom the brief is narrow. You want a low, clean profile that sits close to the mattress height, honest wood, and just enough storage to keep the surface clear. A tall, glossy cabinet or an ornate carved table pulls against the calm before the lamp goes on.

This guide takes the decisions in the order they matter: why a low wooden bedside table suits a minimal room, three starting points, and then height, materials and styling in detail.

Why Choose a Japandi Nightstand?

The appeal is practical as much as visual. A low, simply built nightstand in this style does several things a conventional bedside cabinet does not.

The right height keeps the bedside calm

A nightstand that sits close to the top of the mattress puts the lamp, the glass and the book within easy reach without looming over the bed. That low, level line is a large part of what makes a Japandi bedroom feel restful.

Wood ages instead of dating

Solid oak, ash and walnut develop patina rather than looking worn. A few marks after a year read as character in a wabi-sabi room, where a painted or high-gloss cabinet would simply look tired.

Just enough storage

A drawer hides the small things that gather at the bedside — chargers, hand cream, reading glasses — while an open shelf holds a book or two. That keeps the top surface clear, which is what makes the bedside read as calm rather than busy.

It completes the sleeping group

A nightstand is what turns a bed frame into a finished bedroom. Paired with a low table lamp and a soft rug underfoot, it grounds the whole side of the bed.

How to Choose the Right Nightstand

Size is decided by the bed and the space beside it, not by taste. Get those right and almost any style of nightstand will work.

Match the height to the mattress

The usual guideline is a nightstand top within a couple of inches of the top of the mattress, so you can reach a lamp or a glass without looking down at it. Measure from the floor to the top of your made bed before choosing, since mattress heights vary widely.

Width and the space beside the bed

  • Footprint: measure the gap between the bed and the wall or door. Leave room to walk past and to open a drawer fully.
  • Proportion: a wider nightstand suits a larger bed and a generous room; a narrow one keeps a small bedroom from feeling crowded.
  • A pair or a single: matching nightstands on both sides look calm and balanced, but a single one, or two that simply agree in tone, is equally in keeping.

Drawer, shelf or open

A drawer hides clutter; an open shelf keeps a book to hand and feels lighter; a simple open cube is the most minimal of all. Choose by what you keep at the bedside rather than by looks alone.

Assembly and stability

Many nightstands arrive flat-packed and need assembly, so check what is involved and whether drawers run on smooth runners. A stable top matters more here than on most furniture, since a wobble is obvious when you set a glass down in the dark.

Materials and Craftsmanship

On a piece this small the details show, so construction matters as much as looks. Each material behaves differently over years of daily use.

Solid oak and ash

The default Japandi choice: pale, straight-grained, and warm without being yellow. Solid wood is the most durable option and can be sanded and re-oiled if it marks, which is the main practical argument for paying more than veneer costs.

Walnut and darker woods

A darker nightstand grounds a bedroom with pale walls and light bedding. It shows dust a little more readily than oak, but it brings a quiet depth that suits the style, especially beside a paler bed frame.

Engineered wood and veneer

Many nightstands in the affordable range use engineered wood or a veneer over board. A good veneer looks the same in the room and keeps the cost down; it cannot be sanded back, so check the drawer runners and joints, which is where a cheaper piece gives itself away.

Rattan and cane detailing

Woven rattan or cane on a drawer front adds natural texture that sits comfortably in a Japandi room. It is decorative rather than structural, so make sure the frame and drawer underneath are solidly built.

Handles and drawer pulls

The detail that dates a piece fastest. A recessed or cut-out pull, a small wooden knob, or a plain matte-black handle keeps the look quiet; ornate or shiny hardware works against the calm.

Bedroom Styling Tips

The point of a Japandi nightstand is what you leave off it. A few rules that hold up in a real bedroom:

  • Three things, maximum. A lamp, something with a little height, and one small object. More than that and the surface stops reading as calm.
  • Light it low and warm. A table lamp with a warm bulb gives a soft glow for reading that an overhead light cannot.
  • Add one natural element. A single stem in a small vase or a low plant keeps the bedside feeling alive without cluttering it.
  • Use the drawer. Chargers, cables and hand cream belong inside, not on top — the clear surface is the styling.
  • Echo the bed and the wood. Let the nightstand agree in tone with the bed frame rather than match it exactly, for a look that feels collected rather than bought as a set.
  • Keep the wall above calm. A small piece of wall art or nothing at all keeps the focus on the quiet line of the bedside.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a nightstand “Japandi”?

A low, clean profile close to mattress height, honest natural wood, and just enough storage to keep the top clear. The look is calm and uncluttered, blending Japanese minimalism with Scandinavian warmth, usually with simple hardware or none at all.

How tall should a nightstand be?

Aim for a top within a couple of inches of the top of your mattress, so a lamp or glass is easy to reach. Measure from the floor to the top of your made bed before choosing, as mattress heights vary a lot.

Do my two nightstands need to match?

Not necessarily. A matching pair looks calm and balanced, but two that simply agree in tone and height also work, and a single nightstand is fine in a smaller room or against a wall on one side.

Drawer or open shelf?

A drawer hides clutter and keeps the surface clear; an open shelf feels lighter and keeps a book to hand. Choose by what you keep at the bedside — many Japandi nightstands offer both a drawer and a shelf.

Is solid wood worth it over engineered wood?

For a piece handled every day, solid wood is more durable and can be sanded and re-oiled if it marks. A good engineered-wood or veneer nightstand looks the same in the room for less, so it comes down to budget and how long you expect to keep it.

Should the nightstand match the bed frame?

It should agree in undertone rather than match exactly. A warm oak nightstand sits comfortably beside a warm wood bed; mixing a warm and a cool wood tends to look like a mistake even when both pieces are attractive on their own.

Conclusion

If you get one thing right, get the height right. A simple wooden nightstand level with the mattress will do more for a bedroom than an expensive one that is too tall or too deep. Material decides how it ages; proportion decides whether the bedside feels calm.

Measure the bed and the space beside it, choose just enough storage for what you keep to hand, and leave the top surface mostly clear once it arrives. A nightstand in this style is meant to be used and quietly relied upon, which is the highest compliment furniture like this can be paid.
